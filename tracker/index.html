The tote is hard to beat. On the two held-out seasons (2003–05) the public odds alone explain about as much as any model built here. Benter's two-stage blend puts almost all its weight on the public, so it never finds a positive edge after the 17.5% take.

The market-anchored variant does find a thin tail of overlays at high edge. Its profit there rests on a few hundred bets and the confidence intervals mostly still include losing. Treat flagged runners as candidates to check, not as a proven edge.

How well each view predicts the winner

Pseudo-R², Benter's measure: 1 − log-likelihood ÷ log-likelihood if every runner were equally likely. Test races only.

What the public misprices

Market-anchored weights. A public weight above 1 means favourites win more often than their odds say, the favourite–longshot bias.
